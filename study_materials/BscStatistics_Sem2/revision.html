MODULE SUMMARIES · QUESTION BANK · 7/14-DAY PLANS

Setting priorities

Modules 1–3 each have 15 hours. Module 4 has 30 hours and explicitly requires a practical record with at least 10 problems. The outcomes emphasise understanding random-variable/distribution theory, using distributions, analysing applications, and evaluating fitting/simulation with spreadsheets. The assessment lists 2-, 6- and 12-mark theory answers plus a practical evaluation. The priority labels below follow that evidence and do not predict marks for any one topic.

MUST MASTER

PMF/PDF/CDF, expectation and variance, standard distribution calculations, normal table use, and spreadsheet problem solving.

IMPORTANT

Transformations, joint and conditional distributions, generating functions, memorylessness and model selection.

QUICK-SCORE

Definitions, supports, parameter meanings, standard properties and distribution-identification clues.

  4. PDF
  5. CDF
  6. Univariate transformation
  7. Bivariate random variable
  8. Joint distribution

Key terms

Support · mass · density · cumulative · transformation · joint · marginal · conditional · independent · distribution function

Essential formula prompts

Check total PMF sum or PDF integral is 1; F(x)=P(X≤x); derive marginals by summing/integrating; conditional = joint divided by marginal; independence means joint factors into marginals.

Common mistakes: treating PDF height as point probability; leaving a transformed variable’s support unstated; confusing joint and marginal; treating one matching cell in an independence check as proof of independence.

Expected / Exam-Focused Questions based on the syllabus

  • 2 marks: Define a discrete random variable; state a property of a CDF; define marginal distribution.
  • 6 marks: Explain PMF, PDF and CDF and their properties; obtain marginal/conditional probabilities from a joint table.
  • 12 marks: Given a bivariate distribution, find marginal and conditional distributions and assess independence, showing checks.
  • Numerical: derive a simple CDF from a PMF; transform a discrete variable; calculate marginal and conditional probabilities.

Key terms and formulas

Expected value · raw moment · central moment · variance · standard deviation · MGF · characteristic function · derivative · parameter

E(X)=Σxp(x) or ∫xf(x)dx; Var(X)=E(X²)−[E(X)]²; M(t)=E(eᵗˣ); φ(t)=E(eⁱᵗˣ).

Common mistakes: confusing E(X²) with [E(X)]²; forgetting the square on scaling in Var(aX+b); claiming variance of a sum always adds; mixing up an MGF with a characteristic function.

Expected / Exam-Focused Questions based on the syllabus

  • 2 marks: Define mathematical expectation; state the variance identity; define MGF.
  • 6 marks: Explain expectation and its properties with a simple example; calculate mean and variance from a distribution.
  • 12 marks: Explain expectation, variance and generating functions, then solve a supplied simple distribution problem.
  • Numerical: compute E[g(X)], mean deviation or variance; differentiate a simple MGF to obtain moments.

Discrete and continuous distributions

  3. Binomial
  4. Poisson
  5. Geometric
  6. Geometric lack of memory
  7. Continuous uniform
  8. Exponential

Key terms

Support · parameter · success · rate · shape · density · tail probability · standardisation · z-score · memorylessness

Write the parameter convention: e.g. gamma shape-rate or shape-scale; geometric trials-to-first-success or failures-before-success.

Distribution identification: fixed number of same-chance trials → binomial; count in an interval → Poisson; trials to first success → geometric; continuous wait → exponential; bell-shaped symmetric quantity → normal.

Expected / Exam-Focused Questions based on the syllabus

  • 2 marks: Define Bernoulli distribution; state the mean of Poisson; define standard normal variable.
  • 6 marks: Explain conditions/properties of binomial or Poisson; describe lack of memory for geometric/exponential; compare selected distributions.
  • 12 marks: Discuss listed distributions with their supports, parameters, key properties and applications; solve an appropriate probability problem.
  • Numerical: binomial/Poisson/geometric probabilities; uniform/exponential tail; gamma parameters; normal probabilities using a standard table.

Spreadsheet for statistical computing

10 key practical skills

  1. Enter, label and validate data
  2. Use cell references and ranges
  3. Calculate and check a PMF/CDF
  4. Find marginal probabilities
  5. Calculate expectation and variance
  6. Use a Bernoulli/binomial function
  7. Use Poisson/geometric calculations
  8. Calculate continuous-model probabilities

The record must contain at least 10 problems. For each, include the objective, inputs, formula/function, output, verification and interpretation.

Common mistakes: wrong cell range, mistaken rate/scale, incorrect cumulative vs point-probability function, failing to check that probabilities sum to 1, and assuming one random simulation run must equal theoretical probability.

Checklist

Practice question bank and model answer guidance

Questions are syllabus-aligned practice. They are not actual MGU questions unless an official paper is separately supplied.

2-mark questions

  1. Define a PMF and state its total-probability property.
  2. What is a CDF?
  3. Define mathematical expectation.
  4. State the mean and variance of a Bernoulli variable.
  5. State the lack-of-memory property of the exponential distribution.
  6. What is a standard normal variable?

6-mark questions

  1. Explain the difference between PMF, PDF and CDF.
  2. From a joint PMF table, find marginal and conditional distributions and test independence.
  3. Calculate expectation and variance from a discrete distribution, showing steps.
  4. Compare binomial, Poisson and geometric models, giving a suitable use for each.
  5. Explain how to standardise a normal variable and use the table for a probability.
  6. Describe a spreadsheet method for simulating and checking a binomial probability.

12-mark questions

  1. Explain random variables and their probability functions; include univariate and bivariate examples and properties.
  2. Discuss expectation, variance and generating functions, with a simple worked problem.
  3. Describe the discrete and continuous distributions in the syllabus, including parameters, properties and suitable applications.
  4. Plan a spreadsheet analysis and simulation for a distribution problem; show checks and interpret the output.

Numerical practice

  1. X takes 0,1,2 with probabilities 0.2,0.5,0.3. Find F(0), F(1), E(X), E(X²) and Var(X).
  2. For the joint table in Module 1, find P(X=0), P(Y=0|X=1), and determine whether X and Y are independent.
  3. A fair coin is tossed 5 times. Find the probability of exactly 3 heads.
  4. For a Poisson model with λ=2, find P(X=1) and P(X=0).
  5. For geometric p=0.25 under trials-to-first-success convention, find P(X=3) and E(X).
  6. If X~Uniform(4,10), find P(5<X<8), E(X), and Var(X).
  7. If X is exponential with rate 0.2, find P(X>5).
  8. If X~N(50,8²), standardise x=62 and use the provided table value to obtain a tail probability.

How to shape model answers

2 marks: precise definition plus one key property or example.

6 marks: definition, explanation, formula with symbols, short example or property, and interpretation.

12 marks: organised introduction, concepts and conditions, formulas, stepwise worked problem or diagram, interpretation and concise conclusion.

In numerical answers show: Question → Given → Required → Formula → Substitution → Calculation → Answer → Interpretation.
