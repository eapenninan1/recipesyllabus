Priority and syllabus note

The course has 15 hours for Module 1, 20 for Module 3 and 30 practical hours for Module 4. Module 2 is labelled 10 hours although its sub-unit entries sum to 15. The ESE specifies 2-, 6- and 12-mark theory answers; practical evaluation gives 30 marks for problem-solving and 5 for the record. The practical record minimum is five problems. Priorities follow this evidence rather than guessed topic-wise marks.

Hour discrepancy: please check the intended Module 2 allocation with your lecturer; this material preserves the supplied syllabus figures.
MUST MASTER

Testing procedure and interpretation; the listed z, t, paired t, one-way ANOVA, chi-square and simple non-parametric problems; survey analysis and software skills.

IMPORTANT

Research design, sampling, proposal writing, p-value, power and test assumptions.

QUICK-SCORE

Definitions, hypothesis distinctions, test purpose, error types and conclusion wording.

Scope check

One-way ANOVA only. Sign, median and Wilcoxon ranked tests are limited to simple problems. The supplied syllabus does not specify the Wilcoxon variant.

Testing of hypothesis

10 core concepts

  1. Parameter
  2. Statistic
  3. Simple hypothesis
  4. Composite hypothesis
  5. Null hypothesis
  6. Alternative hypothesis
  7. Type I error
  8. Type II error
  9. Significance level and p-value
  10. Power and critical region

Key terms and reminders

Null model · test statistic · significance level α · p-value · power 1−β · critical region · reject · do not reject

Do not say a p-value is the probability the null hypothesis is true.

Common mistakes: treating “do not reject” as proof; choosing test direction after seeing data; confusing α with p; calling statistical significance practical importance.

Expected / Exam-Focused Questions

  • 2 marks: define parameter, statistic, p-value or significance level.
  • 6 marks: explain Type I and II errors; distinguish simple and composite hypotheses; outline the testing procedure.
  • 12 marks: explain hypothesis testing from claim to contextual decision, including α, p-value, errors and power.

MODULE 3 · 20 HOURS

Parametric and non-parametric tests

10 core concepts

  1. One-mean z test
  2. Two-mean z test
  3. One-sample t test
  4. Independent two-sample t test
  5. Paired t test
  6. One-way ANOVA
  7. Chi-square association test
  8. Sign test
  9. Median test
  10. Wilcoxon ranked procedure

Key terms

Standard error · degrees of freedom · paired difference · pooled variance · between-group variation · expected count · rank · critical value · assumption · association

Choose the procedure: one mean → one-sample z/t; two independent means → two-mean z/t; same units twice → paired t; three or more means with one factor → one-way ANOVA; categorical association → chi-square; simple sign/rank/count setting → the matching non-parametric procedure.

Common mistakes: paired observations treated as independent; ANOVA interpreted as proving all means differ; chi-square used with the wrong expected counts; degrees of freedom omitted; assumptions ignored; exact Wilcoxon variant assumed without checking.

Expected / Exam-Focused Questions

  • 2 marks: define degrees of freedom; state the purpose of one-way ANOVA; define expected frequency.
  • 6 marks: distinguish paired and independent t tests; explain the chi-square test for association; outline one-way ANOVA.
  • 12 marks: select and carry out an appropriate listed test for supplied data, stating hypotheses, method, decision and interpretation.
  • Numerical: compute a z/t statistic; paired differences; ANOVA F components; chi-square statistic; simple sign/median/Wilcoxon-ranked problem.

Question bank and answer-writing guide

These are expected/exam-focused practice questions based on the syllabus, not actual MGU questions.

2-mark questions

  1. Define a parameter and a statistic.
  2. What is a Type I error?
  3. Define a p-value in the context of a test.
  4. What is a paired t test used for?
  5. What is the purpose of one-way ANOVA?
  6. State the chi-square expected-count formula.
  7. Give one difference between qualitative and quantitative research.
  8. What is a critical region?

6-mark questions

  1. Explain the stages of preparing a research proposal.
  2. Explain null/alternative hypotheses, significance level and p-value.
  3. Compare one-sample t and paired t procedures.
  4. Describe one-way ANOVA and its conclusion.
  5. Explain how to test association of attributes using chi-square.
  6. Describe the steps in a social survey project using spreadsheet, R or Python.

12-mark questions

  1. Develop a research plan for a real social issue, including design, sampling, data collection and reporting.
  2. Explain statistical hypothesis testing, error types, significance level, p-value, power and critical region.
  3. For a supplied research problem, choose an appropriate test from the prescribed list, show the procedure and interpret its result.
  4. Describe a social survey and software-based data analysis project, from questionnaire through descriptive summaries and report.

Numerical and commonly confused practice

  • Calculate a one-mean z or t statistic from supplied summary data.
  • Compare two independent means using the procedure and assumptions specified in the question.
  • Calculate paired differences and a paired t statistic.
  • Complete a one-way ANOVA table and interpret F.
  • Find expected cell counts and χ² for a contingency table.
  • Distinguish paired vs independent samples; p-value vs α; statistical vs practical significance; association vs causation.

Model-answer shape

2 marks: definition plus one distinguishing point.

6 marks: definition, explanation, formula/procedure, small example and interpretation.

12 marks: introduction, hypotheses or research objective, method and assumptions, calculation/table, decision, contextual interpretation and limitation/conclusion.

For a test calculation: Given → hypotheses → assumptions/test choice → α → formula → substitution → statistic and p/critical value → decision → conclusion in context.
